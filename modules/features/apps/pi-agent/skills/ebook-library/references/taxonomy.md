# Taxonomy

Reuse these vocabularies unless the library has moved on. They were built for a
mixed fiction and non-fiction library of about 80 books and have held up.

## Tags

Thirty-four words. Every one describes what is inside the book. Add a word only
when it names something a reader would search for, and delete it when it stops
earning its place.

**Genre**
Fantasy, Dark Fantasy, Epic Fantasy, Urban Fantasy, Historical Fantasy, Horror,
Science Fiction, Dystopian, Mystery, Thriller, Romance, Classics, Literary
Fiction

**Subject**
Philosophy, Existentialism, Stoicism, Psychology, Self-Help, Relationships,
Loneliness, Grief, History

**Elements**
Magic, Vampires, Ghosts, Witches, Mythology

**Setting and style**
Dark Academia, Humour

**Audience and form**
Young Adult, Children's, Essays, Short Stories, Omnibus

## Collections

Eighteen sections, named the way a bookshop labels them. A book may sit on two
or three.

Fantasy, Dark Fantasy, Urban Fantasy, Historical Fantasy, Horror, Science
Fiction, Crime & Mystery, Romance, Classics, Literary Fiction, Contemporary
Fiction, Young Adult, Children's, Omnibus, Philosophy, Psychology, Self-Help,
Relationships

`Omnibus` is the one practical shelf. It holds boxed sets, complete series and
collected editions.

## Why these splits

- **Fantasy is split by setting, not by mood.** Urban, historical and dark are
  the distinctions a reader actually makes when choosing.
- **Classics is a publisher-and-canon idea, not an age.** It overlaps freely
  with Horror, Science Fiction and Romance. That overlap is correct.
- **Young Adult cuts across everything.** It is the biggest shelf in a library
  like this, so watch it. If it holds most of the collection, the reader does
  not need it.
- **Genre shelves and genre tags duplicate on purpose in Calibre**, because the
  two are queried differently, and the duplication is worth resolving only once
  the reader browses by shelf. On a Kobo, shelves are the only browsing surface,
  so the tags can afford to be narrower.
- **No mood shelves.** `Cosy & Comforting`, `Cry It Out` and `Dark & Disturbing`
  were tried and rejected: nobody agrees on what belongs in them, and they do
  not survive a second reading of the library.

## Assigning

Work from the description, not from the old tags, which are usually vendor
scrape. Then check the result against these tests.

1. Would someone looking for this kind of book find it? If not, add a shelf.
2. Would someone looking for a different kind of book be annoyed to find it
   here? If so, remove a shelf.
3. Is the tag doing any work a shelf does not? If not, drop the tag.

A worked row:

| Book | Tags | Shelves |
|---|---|---|
| Ninth House | Dark Fantasy, Urban Fantasy, Dark Academia, Horror, Mystery | Dark Fantasy, Urban Fantasy |
| The Adventures of Sherlock Holmes | Classics, Mystery, Short Stories | Classics, Crime & Mystery |
| Attached | Psychology, Relationships, Self-Help | Psychology, Relationships |

Note the third row: `Self-Help` is a tag but not a shelf, because the book is
about attachment rather than general self-improvement. Tags and shelves are
allowed to disagree, as long as the reason is deliberate.

## Books that resist placement

Some titles will not sit still. Decide, state the decision in the reply, and
move on. Do not leave a book with no shelf.

- Dual-timeline literary novels with a speculative thread: pick the shelf the
  blurb leads with, and mention the other.
- Complete-series boxed sets: the `Omnibus` shelf plus the genre, never
  `Young Adult` alone.
- Non-fiction about a fiction-adjacent subject, such as Stoicism: the subject
  shelf, not `Classics`.
- A memoir that is also a historical document: `History` or `Philosophy`,
  whichever the reader would look under.
